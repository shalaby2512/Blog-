Research focus

From working prototype to defensible research

Primary research question

How can platform deployment, interface-centred integration and evidence-led fault diagnosis transfer a low-cost ROS 2 follow-me cart from simulation to physical hardware while maintaining predictable motion and fail-safe behaviour?

01

Physical deployment

Deployed ROS 2 Jazzy on the Raspberry Pi 5 and verified the LiDAR, UWB, odometry, IMU, safety and Arduino interfaces as one system.

02

Failure containment

All motion requests pass through the LiDAR safety supervisor. Stale scans, lost targets and expired requests produce a stop in the tested prototype.

03

Measured follow trials

Six post-fix runs totalled 297 seconds. Hold error was target range minus 1.02 m while commanded linear speed was zero; the six runs varied in accuracy.

Evidence boundary: The main series contains six short runs in a small university laboratory. The payload comparison is a separate pair with only one short loaded run. Neither supports a statistical payload effect, certified collision avoidance or long-duration reliability.

ROS 2 Jazzy deployment

Deployed the modular stack on the Raspberry Pi 5 and verified its sensor, safety, telemetry and motor boundaries in the physical build.

Motor-control integration

Integrated Ashwin’s v3 firmware command protocol and base controller with Arduino telemetry, bounded PWM and zero-command handling.

Safety and recovery verification

Verified the shared BOOT, READY, FOLLOW, SLOW, STOP and RECOVER behaviour on hardware, including stale-scan STOP and target reacquisition hold.

Software integration

Connected the team’s ranging, estimation, follow control, safety supervisor and serial bridge through explicit ROS 2 topics and a single launch flow.

Fault diagnosis

Led diagnosis of Pi undervoltage and UWB reset failures through load-side measurements and startup tests; the L298N turning fault was diagnosed jointly by the team.

Evidence and authorship

Maintained deployment evidence and supported end-to-end validation. Ashwin designed and implemented the EKF and gates, follow controller, safety node, no-reverse base rule and v3 firmware command interface; I integrated those components on hardware. Vignesh designed and fabricated the chassis.

August 2026 · Hardware milestone

The Trolley-X physical platform takes shape

These August build photos document the initial chassis and L298N driver arrangement. Later floor testing found major driver voltage loss, so the final tested prototype used two BTS7960 bridges, a separate USB-C supply for the Raspberry Pi 5 and a 3S LiPo motor rail. The four geared motors, front encoders, RPLIDAR A1, two UWB anchors and MPU6050 IMU were integrated with ROS 2 Jazzy and Arduino control.

August bring-up record

Three early embedded tests

Test 01

Open-loop motor run

Commands forward, reverse and zero-radius left/right turns at PWM 120, with an explicit stop between each two-second movement. This verifies driver pin mapping and basic skid-steer behaviour.

Download Arduino test →
Test 02

Encoder verification

Uses hardware interrupts to count rising edges on both front encoders and the B channels to infer direction. Counts are reported every 500 ms to expose polarity or wiring errors.

Download Arduino test →
Test 03

Closed-loop correction

Converts unequal encoder counts into distance using 332 left and 325 right ticks per revolution, then applies proportional PWM correction at 20 Hz to reduce straight-line distance error.

Download Arduino test →
Engineering note

These downloadable sketches document the early L298N bring-up. The file named “PID test” implements proportional distance balancing, without integral or derivative terms. The final integrated follow stack used a later blended differential-drive command interface and BTS7960 drivers; these sketches are retained as historical evidence, not presented as final firmware.

ROS 2–Arduino integration

Deployed ROS 2 Jazzy on the Raspberry Pi 5, connected Arduino commands and telemetry, and verified odometry, IMU, UWB and LiDAR topic boundaries. All autonomous and manual motion requests pass through the safety supervisor.

September 2026
Completed

Power and UWB startup faults resolved

The Pi supply fell from 5.05 V idle to 4.61 V under full load. A separate USB-C supply held 4.90–4.99 V. A roughly 150 ms NRST boot pulse removed the UWB modules’ manual-reset dependency.

September 2026
Completed

Loaded turning and spin-lock corrected

The L298N delivered only about 2.2 V at the motors during a 63% duty spin. The team changed to BTS7960 bridges and reduced tyre scrub. Blended differential drive, validity gates and a 0.7 s reacquisition hold addressed the early spin-lock.

17–21 September 2026
Completed

Six integrated follow trials analysed

Six post-fix runs totalled 297 s. Mean hold MAE was 16.7 ± 7.0 cm across runs, with a range of 8.7–25.8 cm. The 8.7 cm MAE / 11.0 cm RMSE result was the 32 s run. Matched-log filtering reduced bearing jitter by 75–87%. Hold error uses target range minus 1.02 m only while commanded linear speed is zero.

September 2026
Completed

Exploratory 4 kg comparison

Recorded a separate no-payload/loaded pair. Mean encoder speed during driving fell from 0.202 to 0.153 m/s, while mean commanded speed changed from 0.256 to 0.233 m/s. Details and limits appear below.

Quantified safety and endurance

Measure end-to-end sensor-to-deceleration latency, speed-dependent stopping distance, nuisance stops and longer runs across obstacle types, trajectories, measured cart mass, repeated payloads and compute loads. Return-to-home remains optional and unimplemented.

What the measurements mean

Static two-anchor ranging reached 3.3 cm RMSE over 0.75–3.0 m, but that is sensor accuracy, not whole-cart following accuracy. The six runs were short and used a small laboratory area; obstacle state transitions were observed, while a controlled stopping-distance matrix was not completed. The original open-loop versus proportional straight-line drift comparison was not reported as a controlled final result.

September 2026 · Exploratory load test

What changed with a 4 kg payload?

A separate no-payload run was compared with one 4 kg run. Both used the same hold-error method as the six-run series: target range minus 1.02 m, sampled only when commanded linear speed was zero. The logged dist_hold_err_m column was not used. Mean achieved speed came from encoder odometry during driving.

Reading the result

The loaded cart’s mean achieved driving speed was about 24% lower, although the mean commands were similar rather than identical. Hold bias shifted from −11.2 to +14.3 cm, consistent with lag behind the operator. NIS and jitter summaries show no clear estimator failure in this pair, so the drive response is the likely bottleneck observed here. This is an indication from one loaded run, not a statistical or isolated causal result: that run was 28 s long with about 12.8 s of driving, and the cart mass was not measured.

Safety observation: No safety events were recorded during the follow portions; minimum front distance was 0.71 m. During loading, a person close to the cart’s front caused LiDAR STOP as designed. This was not a controlled moving-obstacle stopping test.
